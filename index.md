# Wide Open Privacy Policy

_Last updated: October 20, 2026_

This policy covers the Android app published as **Wide Open** (package `com.treyfishing.app`).

## Summary
The app has no ads. An account is optional, and you must be 18 or older to make one. Without one, nothing leaves your phone. If you make an account, we keep your email, a username, an optional name, bio and picture, who follows whom, and your Private setting. Sign-in uses Supabase, our account service, which stores your email and issues a sign-in token; the token is kept on your phone, outside backups, so you stay signed in. Google sign-in shares only your email with Wide Open; we do not copy your Google name or picture. The private log, gear and maps work with no account and no signal. You can delete the account any time in Account > Delete account, which removes your profile, followers and following, and anything you published. We never store your location, wallet or ID documents, contacts or private notes. We do not sell personal data. Everything you log is private and stays on your phone until you publish it. In this version your location is not sent to us, and the only thing sent to anyone else is the two river points you pin for the Float timer, once, to the USGS, when you tap Save. The only positions the app keeps are a catch pin you choose to save, a map pin you drop and name, the spots of a run you plan or save, a Float timer put-in and take-out, and a boat trail recorded during a trip while the app's trip notification shows (on by default for public charter trips, off for private trips, and you can turn it off). Nothing you enter leaves your phone unless you send it yourself, or Android backup copies it to your own Google account.

## Private and public
Everything you enter in Wide Open is private: trips, catches, catch pins, map pins, boat trails, planned and saved runs and their spots, Lookout finds, Lines in times, boats, spots, notes, photos, gear and settings. Currently there is no public profile, feed or map, and nothing you log is posted anywhere or shown to other anglers. Where the app says "Public charter", that is only a kind of trip (a boat you book a spot on), not a sharing setting. The only public information in the app is information that was already public, such as NOAA and weather data, the landings directory, regulations and the species and knots guides. If we ever add a way to share your catches or trips with others, it will be off for every item until you choose to share that item, it will show you exactly what others will see first, it will never show your pins (catch pins or map pins), spot notes or position, and this policy will be updated before it arrives.

## Data you enter
Trips, packing lists, tackle, boats, map pins, planned and saved runs, and catch logs (including photos you choose) are stored only on your device. Wide Open's servers never receive them; an optional account keeps only its profile and what you choose to publish. If you uninstall the app or clear its data, they are deleted. You can export your own data as CSV from Settings; the export does not include catch pins, map pins, boat trails, boats or runs. You can also save a backup file from Settings to move to a new phone. A backup holds your log, including catch pins, map pins, boat trails, planned and saved runs and photos, and goes only where you save it, so keep it somewhere private. If you use Android's backup, Google may back up the app's database, including saved catch pins, map pins, boat trails and runs, under your own Google account settings; photos and the app's usage counts are not part of that backup. Lookout finds, Lines in times and the boat link's address stay out of backup files and Android's cloud backup (see those sections below).

**People you met.** If you save someone you met on a trip, it stays on your phone with your other logs.

## Photos
When you attach a photo to a catch, you can take one with your phone's camera app or choose existing ones in Android's system photo picker. The app receives only the photo you take or the photos you select. The app asks for camera permission only when you use live measuring (Measure a fish) or take a catch photo. The live picture is processed on your phone and is not saved or sent unless you save the photo with a catch. It requests no broad photo permission. If you tap "Use photo's location", the app asks for Android's photo location permission (ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION) so it can read the place the photo was taken from the photo itself. That position stays on your device and is saved only if you save it as the catch pin. A photo you take is kept briefly in the app's private cache and removed as soon as the catch's copy is made; saved photos never carry a location. A photo you take with the camera needs no photo location permission, and if location is already allowed the app may offer your phone's position from when you took it, which you still have to tap to use. "Choose original" opens your files so a photo keeps the location Android's photo picker can hide.

Live measuring uses Google's ARCore, which runs on your phone. Its use is subject to [Google's Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

## Microphone
"Say a catch" lets you log a catch by talking. It appears only on phones with Android's on-device speech recognizer (Android 12 and newer), and the app never uses an online recognizer. The first time you use it, the app asks for microphone permission (RECORD_AUDIO). The microphone is used only while you hold the button, or until you stop talking after a tap. The listening is done by Android's built-in speech recognizer, part of your phone's system, not by Wide Open's own code. It turns your words into text on your phone, and the app reads that text to fill in the catch, which you check and save yourself. The sound is never recorded, saved or sent, and the words are not kept after the catch is filled in. If your phone needs Android's offline English voice pack, Android downloads it from Google once, when you tap Download voice pack; the app sends nothing with it.

## Sharing
The catch share card is created only when you tap Share. It goes to the app you choose in Android's share sheet and does not include catch pins, map pins, boat trails or run spots. The app does not share anything on its own. Three other actions hand information to another app only when you start them: Add to calendar sends the trip's title, place and notes to your calendar app, opening a catch pin or a map pin opens it in your maps app, and Add to phone contacts opens your own Contacts app with the details filled in. You decide whether to save. Wide Open never asks for or reads your contacts. You can also keep up to five people on shore in the check-in and message them from a trip, with a map picture of where you are if you choose one. The app only opens the message in your own messages app after you see a preview, and you send it yourself.

## Network requests
The app has the INTERNET permission to download public conditions data (such as NOAA buoy, tide, satellite ocean temperature, chlorophyll and surface current data, National Weather Service forecasts, and Open-Meteo weather). These requests use the preset coordinates (or preset station and office IDs) of the fishing area you follow, or the listed position of a landing in the app's directory for that landing's weather alerts, not your phone's location, and they do not identify you. As with any web request, the servers that answer can see your device's IP address, and the app does not send them anything else about you. No account, advertising ID or personal details are sent. The optional boat link also uses that permission, but only on your boat's own local network (see Boat link and boats nearby). Links to outside websites (landings and booking pages, official fishing regulations and species sources) open in your browser and are governed by those sites' own policies; map links open your maps app. The maps inside the app are drawn from map files that ship with the app (coastline, place names, roads and depth lines), which need no network. Only if you turn on the "Online street map" switch in Settings (it is off by default) and your phone has a connection does the map load OpenStreetMap map tiles from OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org) for the area on screen. As you pan and zoom, that service can see the area you are viewing, along with your device's IP address and the app's name, but never your catches or anything you typed, and never your phone's position: when you tap My location on a map, that map switches to the built-in map until you tap Recenter, so no map detail is ever loaded around you. Maps of your catch pins, maps that show your map pins, and the run planner's maps never load online tiles. With the switch off, the map never contacts OpenFreeMap. Without a connection the map always falls back to the built-in map. The Float timer, on a river trip, is the one place the app sends points you chose: if you pin a put-in and a take-out, then when you tap Save the app sends those two points to the U.S. Geological Survey's river network (api.water.usgs.gov) once for that pair, to measure the river miles between them along the water. The request carries nothing else about you or the trip, and the USGS can see your device's IP address as with any web request. Typing the miles instead sends nothing. The Float timer also asks the USGS for a gauge's readings and past field measurements using only the gauge's number. The other exception is the ocean layers (water temperature, chlorophyll or current) on a trip map: when you switch one on, the app asks NOAA CoastWatch for public data in a box around that trip's fishing area, using the area's preset coordinates. NOAA's servers can therefore see which fishing area you are looking at, along with your device's IP address, but never your phone's position or the part of the map you are viewing.

## Location
Location is optional. The app may offer location access when you first open it, and you can decline; you can change this later in Settings or by tapping a location button. Your phone's location is used on your device only. It is read when you tap "Use my location": to set a catch pin, to find the nearest fishing area for Conditions, to choose the area for a trip, and to find your fishing regulations area. It is also read when you start a new trip if you have already allowed location access, to preselect the nearest fishing area. Apart from the boat trail (below), the app never reads your location with the screen off and keeps no location history or tracks. A position is saved only when you save a catch pin, drop a map pin or set a run spot, or while a boat trail records, and it is never sent to us or anyone else. Picking the nearest area uses the position once and does not save it. On a trip map, the "My position" layer reads your location continuously while it is switched on and the map screen is showing, and stops when you leave the screen or put the app in the background. That position is held in memory only to draw the dot and the coordinates under the map: it is never saved, and never sent to us or anyone else. The one exception to "never sent" is the Float timer's two river points, described in Network requests and Float timer: you choose them, and they go only when you tap Save.

## Boat trail
During a trip, the app can record where the boat goes: a position about once a minute, with its time, while that trip's notification ("On the water") is showing. It is on by default for public charter trips and off for private trips until you turn it on. While it records, the app says "Boat trail on" with a Turn off button (Pause on a trip you turned it on for), and the notification says "Trail on" with a Pause trail button. It stops when the trip ends, when the boat has been out on the water for about five minutes and the phone is then well inland, away from the shore, for about three minutes (the app works that out on your phone from the coastline built into it, and says so; Resume starts it again), when the notification goes (including if you swipe it away; opening the app again during the trip brings back the notification and the trail with it), or when you pause it or turn it off for the trip or for every trip in Settings. It needs precise location while you use the app and the notification; it does not use Android's background location permission and never records outside a trip. The trail is drawn on your trip map and your Catches map using only the coastline built into the app, never online map tiles, and is used to suggest a pin for a catch you logged without one, which is saved only if you tap Use this spot. Like a catch pin, a boat trail stays on your phone, is never sent to us, is never on a share card, in a CSV export or in the app's usage counts, and is included in backups. If the end of a trail runs over land, the trip map offers to trim it to the water, which removes those points from your phone a few seconds later unless you tap Undo. It is deleted with its trip, and you can delete one trail or all of them any time.

## Back to the dock
Man overboard, Go to and the anchor alarm are optional helpers on boat trips, off until you start one. While one is running the app keeps reading your phone's position, with a notification showing, so it can show the way back or sound the alarm even with the screen off. It needs precise location while you use the app; it does not use Android's background location permission, is started only from the open app, and stops when you stop the last helper. Your position stays on the phone: it is used to work out distance, direction and whether the anchor has moved, and it is never sent to us or anyone else. The only position the app saves is the Man overboard marker, which is a normal private map pin that you can delete. While a helper runs, the app also keeps what is needed to resume it after a restart (the place you are heading to, or the anchor's spot and ring size) in its settings on your phone, and clears it when you stop. That is not in backup files or Android's cloud backup, and it is never in a share card, CSV export, feedback or the app's usage counts. These are helpers, not rescue devices: they need the phone on, charged, with GPS and the app running.

## Run planner
You can save boats (name, length, speed, fuel burn and tank) and plan a trip's run: where you leave from, a spot you tap on the app's built-in map, and how long you plan to fish. The app works out the distance, heading, run time and fuel on your phone. The spot is a saved position, kept like a catch pin: it stays on your phone, is never sent to us, is never on a share card, in a CSV export or in the app's usage counts, and is included in backups. You can also save a run to your own list of runs and keep it, with no trip attached, until you delete it; its spots are kept the same way. A trip's run is a copy of a saved run, so deleting either never changes the other. The run planner's map uses only the coastline built into the app and never loads online map tiles. The planner does not read your phone's location.

## Drift planner
The Drift planner works out, from the saved forecast, where a drift would take your boat. It runs on your phone and sends nothing. It keeps two small things: the numbers it learns from your own drifts (how fast your boat moves with the wind, per boat and drift sock size, how many drifts that came from, and the day, never a position, a track or a time of day) and, if you log a catch during a drift, the drift speed saved with that catch. These stay on your phone and are never sent anywhere. Like your boats and catches, they are included in your backup file and in Android's backup to your Google account if you have that on. They are not on a share card, in a CSV export or in the app's usage counts. Drift alerts use the position the app already reads while a trip shows On the water, and nothing more.

## Map pins
You can drop your own pin on a map, by holding a finger on the built-in map or with the Drop pin button, and give it a name, description, icon and color. The app never fills one in from your phone's position or a photo. A map pin is a saved position, kept like a catch pin: it stays on your phone, is never sent to us or any server, is never on a share card, in a CSV export, in feedback or in the app's usage counts, and is included in backups. It leaves your phone only if you open it in your own maps app. Maps that show your pins draw only the built-in map and never load online tiles. You can delete a pin any time.

## Boat link and boats nearby
Boat link is optional. If you connect Wide Open to your boat's Wi-Fi gateway, it reads live boat data (position, depth, heading, speed, water temperature, engine) over your boat's local network to show on screen. It never writes to your boat's network, and the readings aren't saved or sent anywhere. It uses the network access the app already has, so Android asks for no extra permission for this. The app keeps only the gateway's address and your boat link choices in its settings on your phone; they are left out of backup files and Android's cloud backup, though Android's phone-to-phone transfer carries them with your other settings.

Boats nearby (AIS) is also optional. With a boat link to a boat that has AIS, the app shows nearby vessels' public AIS reports on the map. They are read from your boat's network, never from the internet, and are not saved or sent anywhere.

## Lookout
Lookout is an optional game, off until you turn it on. It reads no position of its own and never uses Android's background location permission. When you open On the water, it compares the boat trail's already saved positions with a built-in list of islands and headlands and with a formula that places make-believe finds on a grid, and keeps only the item or landmark, its fishing area and the day, never a position. It collects only while the boat trail is on, the lines are out and the boat is running, and nothing from before you turned it on. To avoid collecting a find twice, it also keeps a short list of the grid squares (about 3 nautical miles across) already collected today and yesterday, the time you turned it on, and how far along each trip's trail it has looked. All of this stays in the app's settings on your phone: it is never sent anywhere, never on a share card, in a CSV export, in feedback or in the app's usage counts, and is not in backup files or Android's cloud backup (Android's phone-to-phone transfer carries it with your other settings). A trip's finds go when the trip is deleted, and Delete Lookout finds removes them all. Finds are make-believe and say nothing about where fish are.

## Lines in
The Lines in switch keeps the times you said your lines were in, for each trip. It keeps no position: it only reads the boat trail to notice a run (the boat at 8 knots or more for 10 minutes) and never changes when the trail records. Those times stay in the app's settings on your phone and are dropped with the trip. They are never sent anywhere, never on a share card, in a CSV export, in feedback or in the app's usage counts, and are not in backup files or Android's cloud backup (Android's phone-to-phone transfer carries them with your other settings).

## Float timer
On a river trip, the Float timer estimates how long a float from a put-in to a take-out takes, from the miles and the flow of the water's USGS gauge. You can type the miles, which sends nothing, or pin a put-in and a take-out on the app's built-in map. When you tap Save on pins, the app sends those two points, and nothing else, to the USGS river network (api.water.usgs.gov) once for that pair to measure the miles along the water. The pins and the miles stay on your phone with the trip, in the app's settings: they are not in Android's cloud backup or phone-to-phone transfer, they are never on a share card, in a CSV export, in feedback or in the app's usage counts, they go when the trip is deleted for good, and Erase all data removes them. The wind along the river is worked out on your phone from a forecast already saved for the trip's water. The estimate is a planning guide, not a promise.

## Feedback
Settings has a Send feedback screen. A report you write is saved on your phone and, when you choose to send it, it opens your own email app with the text filled in. The app itself sends nothing; you decide whether to send the email. Giving contact details is optional, and you can choose whether to include a line with the app version, Android version and phone model.

## Notifications
Notifications are optional. The app asks for notification permission only when you turn one on: "Show on lock screen" on the On the water card, "Trip on the lock screen" in Settings, or trip reminders. The trip notification shows your current trip on the lock screen; if your lock screen hides sensitive content, it shows only "On the water" and the back time (plus "Trail on" while a boat trail records, or "Trail stopped, back on land" after it stops by itself), and it goes away when the trip ends. Trip reminders are scheduled on your phone for trips you planned, and are set again after the phone restarts. Both are built on your phone, and nothing about them is sent anywhere.

## Ads and analytics
This version contains no advertising and no advertising or tracking code. It keeps simple counts of which features you use on your device to help improve the app; currently these counts are not sent anywhere.

## Children
The app is not directed to children and currently collects no data from anyone.

## Changes
This policy describes how Wide Open works in the version you have today. Wide Open may change over time, for example by adding optional features such as backup or sharing. If a change affects what data the app collects, stores off your phone or shares, we will update this policy and its date before that version is released, and any new feature that sends data will be off until you choose to use it. Using a later version means the updated policy applies to it.
