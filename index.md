# Wide Open Privacy Policy

_Last updated: October 7, 2026_

This policy covers the Android app published as **Wide Open** (package `com.treyfishing.app`).

## Summary
The app has no accounts and no ads. It does not collect, share or sell personal data. Everything you enter is private and stays on your phone. Your location is never sent to us or anyone else, and the only position the app keeps is a catch pin you choose to save. Nothing you enter leaves your phone unless you send it yourself, or Android backup copies it to your own Google account.

## Private and public
Everything you enter in Wide Open is private: trips, catches, catch pins, spots, notes, photos, gear and settings. There is no public profile, feed or map, and nothing you log is posted anywhere or shown to other anglers. Where the app says "Public charter", that is only a kind of trip (a boat you book a spot on), not a sharing setting. The only public information in the app is information that was already public, such as NOAA and weather data, the landings directory, regulations and the species and knots guides. If we ever add a way to share your catches or trips with others, it will be off for every item until you choose to share that item, it will show you exactly what others will see first, it will never show your pins, spot notes or position, and this policy will be updated before it arrives.

## Data you enter
Trips, packing lists, tackle, and catch logs (including photos you choose) are stored only on your device. We have no servers and cannot see them. If you uninstall the app or clear its data, they are deleted. You can export your own data as CSV from Settings; the export does not include catch pins. You can also save a backup file from Settings to move to a new phone. A backup holds everything, including catch pins and photos, and goes only where you save it, so keep it somewhere private. If you use Android's backup, Google may back up the app's database, including saved catch pins, under your own Google account settings; photos and the app's usage counts are not part of that backup.

## Photos
When you attach a photo to a catch, you can take one with your phone's camera app or choose existing ones in Android's system photo picker. The app receives only the photo you take or the photos you select. It requests no broad photo or camera permission. If you tap "Use photo's location", the app asks for Android's photo location permission (ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION) so it can read the place the photo was taken from the photo itself. That position stays on your device and is saved only if you save it as the catch pin. A photo you take is kept briefly in the app's private cache and removed as soon as the catch's copy is made; saved photos never carry a location. A photo you take with the camera needs no photo location permission, and if location is already allowed the app may offer your phone's position from when you took it, which you still have to tap to use. "Choose original" opens your files so a photo keeps the location Android's photo picker can hide.

## Sharing
The catch share card is created only when you tap Share. It goes to the app you choose in Android's share sheet and does not include catch pins. The app does not share anything on its own. Two other actions hand information to another app only when you start them: Add to calendar sends the trip's title, place and notes to your calendar app, and opening a catch pin opens it in your maps app.

## Network requests
The app has the INTERNET permission to download public conditions data (such as NOAA buoy, tide, satellite ocean temperature, chlorophyll and surface current data, National Weather Service forecasts, and Open-Meteo weather). These requests use the preset coordinates (or preset station and office IDs) of the fishing area you follow, or the listed position of a landing in the app's directory for that landing's weather alerts, not your phone's location, and they do not identify you. As with any web request, the servers that answer can see your device's IP address, and the app does not send them anything else about you. No account, advertising ID or personal details are sent. Links to outside websites (landings and booking pages, official fishing regulations and species sources) open in your browser and are governed by those sites' own policies; map links open your maps app. The maps inside the app are first drawn from a coastline file that ships with the app, which needs no network. When your phone has a connection and the "Detailed online map" switch in Settings is on (it is on by default), the map also loads OpenStreetMap map tiles from OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org) for the area on screen. As you pan and zoom, that service can see the area you are viewing, along with your device's IP address and the app's name, but never your catches or anything you typed, and never your phone's position: when you tap My location on a map, that map switches to the built-in coastline until you tap Recenter, so no map detail is ever loaded around you. Maps of your catch pins never load online tiles. Turn the switch off to use only the built-in coastline, and the map never contacts OpenFreeMap. Without a connection the map always falls back to the built-in coastline. The other exception is the ocean layers (water temperature, chlorophyll or current) on a trip map: when you switch one on, the app asks NOAA CoastWatch for public data in a box around that trip's fishing area, using the area's preset coordinates. NOAA's servers can therefore see which fishing area you are looking at, along with your device's IP address, but never your phone's position or the part of the map you are viewing.

## Location
Location is optional. The app may offer location access when you first open it, and you can decline; you can change this later in Settings or by tapping a location button. Your phone's location is used on your device only. It is read when you tap "Use my location": to set a catch pin, to find the nearest fishing area for Conditions, to choose the area for a trip, and to find your fishing regulations area. It is also read when you start a new trip if you have already allowed location access, to preselect the nearest fishing area. The app never reads your location in the background and keeps no location history or tracks. A position is saved only when you save a catch pin, and it is never sent to us or anyone else. Picking the nearest area uses the position once and does not save it. On a trip map, the "My position" layer reads your location continuously while it is switched on and the map screen is showing, and stops when you leave the screen or put the app in the background. That position is held in memory only to draw the dot and the coordinates under the map: it is never saved, and never sent to us or anyone else.

## Feedback
Settings has a Send feedback screen. A report you write is saved on your phone and, when you choose to send it, it opens your own email app with the text filled in. The app itself sends nothing; you decide whether to send the email. Giving contact details is optional, and you can choose whether to include a line with the app version, Android version and phone model.

## Notifications
Notifications are optional. The app asks for notification permission only when you turn one on: "Show on lock screen" on the On the water card, "Trip on the lock screen" in Settings, or trip reminders. The trip notification shows your current trip on the lock screen; if your lock screen hides sensitive content, it shows only "On the water" and the back time, and it goes away when the trip ends. Trip reminders are scheduled on your phone for trips you planned, and are set again after the phone restarts. Both are built on your phone, and nothing about them is sent anywhere.

## Ads and analytics
The app contains no advertising. It keeps simple counts of which features you use on your device to help improve the app; these counts are not sent anywhere.

## Children
The app is not directed to children and collects no data from anyone.
